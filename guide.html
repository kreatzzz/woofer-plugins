Getting started

Download and first open

Get an installer from the releases page: one macOS download for Apple Silicon and Intel, an installer or a plain zip for Windows, tarballs or a package manager on Linux. The macOS build is unsigned for now, so macOS blocks the first open: right-click Woofer and choose Open — on recent macOS, System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway. Every later launch is an ordinary double-click.

Sign in

Press Sign in with Spotify. Your browser opens Spotify's own consent page; your password never touches Woofer. The browser is needed once per machine — the refresh token lives in your platform's state directory. Browsing and search work on any account; playing needs Spotify Premium, and a Free account is told so at sign-in.

Enable playback on this computer

Playing here takes one more browser approval, because Spotify treats streaming as a separate grant from the catalog API. Take it from the device menu (Play here, set up once) or from Settings. After that, this computer is a Spotify Connect device named Woofer — visible from your phone like any speaker — and it can also wake receivers on your network that Spotify cannot see yet.

Worth knowing

  • Closing the window does not stop the music. The tray keeps playing and brings the window back; quit from the tray menu or Ctrl+Q.
  • Space plays and pauses, Ctrl+F or / searches, Q opens the queue, and Ctrl+/ lists every shortcut there is.

Lyrics

The microphone button in the player bar (or L) opens lyrics beside the page. Synced lines highlight and scroll with the song; click a line to seek there. Scrolling by hand pauses the following, and Follow resumes it. Lyrics come from Spotify when local playback is authorized, otherwise from LRCLIB, an open database that needs no account. Podcasts and untranscribed tracks say so honestly.

Translation and romanization

Two toggles, in the panel header and in Settings → Lyrics. Romanize rewrites the line in Latin letters: Japanese becomes romaji, Korean becomes romanized hangul. Translate echoes the line in your language, small and quiet under the original; the language picker defaults to English.

Both jobs are plugin jobs. Translate and Romanize ship inside the app; a plugin from the catalog can take either one over, and when more than one provider is installed, the Plugins page asks which one you want.

Plugins

A plugin is one .wasm file that computes. Woofer fetches everything for it and holds it to the domains its manifest declares — no socket, no file, no clock crosses the boundary, and the app is fully functional with zero plugins.

Installing

From the catalog, press Open in Woofer: the deep link lands in the running app, which asks for a confirmation before installing. (The link needs Woofer running.) Without the app open, download the .wasm and drag it onto the Plugins page, or paste its address under Install from URL.

Writing your own

The handbook is plugins/README.md in the main repository; the design is docs/plugins.md. The woofer-plugin-sdk wires the ABI for you and runs your module offline on the same interpreter the host uses. A plugin is two pure functions: plan decides what to fetch, fulfil reads what came back. When it works, propose it to the catalog with a pull request — the CONTRIBUTING.md in the catalog repository has the whole flow.
